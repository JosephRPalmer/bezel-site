Use the whole front screen.

On the iPhone Duo front screen, Safari's toolbar leaves an empty strip beside every web page. Bezel is a Safari extension that widens pages into it, so you see more of the page and less blank space.

Coming soon to the App Store.

For iPhone Duo. No tracking, accounts or ads.

Why Bezel

  • More page, every page

    Text, photos and maps run to the edge of the screen instead of stopping at an empty margin.

  • Careful by default

    In Auto, Bezel checks each page after it loads. If widening would hide anything or make the page scroll sideways, it leaves that page alone.

  • Private

    No accounts, no analytics, no servers. Your choices stay on your iPhone. Read the privacy policy.

Choose per site

Long-press the small Bezel button on any page to pick a mode for that site.

  • Auto
    Widens when it's safe. The default.
  • Wide
    Always widens this site.
  • Normal
    Never widens this site.

Set up in a minute

  1. Get Bezel from the App Store and open it once.
  2. Turn on the extension. Bezel takes you to its Safari settings. Tap All Websites and choose Allow.
  3. Open the test page. It confirms Bezel is working. After that, just browse.

Support

If pages aren't widening:

  1. Open the Bezel app once. It records your screen's layout so the extension knows when to run.
  2. Check the extension is allowed. In Bezel's Safari settings, All Websites should be set to Allow.
  3. Open the test page. It shows straight away whether Bezel is working.

Bezel only widens pages on the iPhone Duo front screen in portrait. On a site that looks wrong, long-press the Bezel button and choose Normal.

Privacy policy

Last updated 10 November 2026

In short: Bezel collects nothing. It has no accounts, no analytics and no servers. What it remembers stays on your iPhone.

Who we are

Bezel is made by QCQM. This policy covers the Bezel app for iPhone, its Safari extension, and this website.

What Bezel keeps on your iPhone

  • Site names. The name of each site you visit, such as example.com, never the full address, page title or content.
  • Your choices. The mode you picked for each site (Auto, Wide or Normal), your default mode, and whether you hid the Bezel button on a site.
  • Auto's notes. When Auto leaves a site alone, a short reason and time, so it can try again later.

This is stored in Safari's extension storage and in the app's own storage on your iPhone, so the app and the extension can share your choices. Sites you have only visited, without choosing a mode, are kept for the 200 most recent and removed after 90 days unseen. You can reset a site in the app, or delete everything with Reset all in Settings. Deleting the app removes it all.

What Bezel reads on web pages

To widen a page, the Safari extension needs permission to run on websites. It uses that permission only to adjust the page's width and to measure the page's layout, the sizes and positions of things on screen, so Auto can tell whether widening is safe. Apart from the site names above, it keeps nothing from the pages you visit: no full addresses, page text, passwords or anything you type.

What Bezel sends

Nothing. Bezel makes no network requests of its own. It has no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking, and shares nothing with QCQM or anyone else. The app links to our test page, which opens in Safari like any other website.

This website

This site and the test page use no cookies, analytics or trackers. They are hosted on GitHub Pages, which may keep standard server logs, such as IP addresses, for security. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Children

Bezel collects no personal information from anyone, including children.
