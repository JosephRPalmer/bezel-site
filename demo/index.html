Three days on the coast path

Forty miles of headlands, harbour towns and one very good bakery, walked slowly with a light pack.

Words and pictures by the Long Weekend desk · 8 min read

The path leaves the harbour along a sea wall still dark from the morning tide. Within half an hour the town is behind you, and the only sounds are gulls, the wind in the gorse and your own boots on the chalk.

We planned the walk around three nights in small inns, each about thirteen miles apart. That is a comfortable day with time to stop: long enough to feel you have travelled, short enough to arrive before the kitchen closes.

“By the second afternoon you stop checking the map and start reading the coast instead.”

Day two · The long headland

Day one: the cove below the old coastguard cottages, the harbour at Wrenmouth, and the bakery that justified the detour.

The second day is the hardest and the best. The path climbs steeply out of the valley, then runs for miles along the top of the cliffs with nothing between you and the horizon. Carry more water than you think you need; there is no shop until the far end.

The final day is gentler, dropping through farmland into a wide estuary where the ferry runs every half hour. Time the last mile to catch it, and you will finish with your feet up, watching the far bank come slowly towards you.
